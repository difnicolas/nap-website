Kingdom 1886

Rules & Punishments

The list below is the official set of NAP violations. It applies to every player in a NAP alliance, including farm and academy alliances. Not knowing the list is not a defence — R4s and R5s are expected to pass it on.

Violations

Each violation carries what the council applies at each level. What the trials cost is set out underneath.

1

Attacking a NAP member's Town Centre

No solo hits and no rallies against the city of any player in a NAP alliance, at any time, regardless of power, alliance or how new they are. This includes players who are shieldless, offline or newly migrated in.

Resources returned to the victim, troops healed, an apology in kingdom chat, and Demotion to R1 for one day.

Resources returned, Demotion to R1 for one day, plus an Arrogance Trial of one day.

Resources returned, Demotion to R1 for one day, an Avarice Trial of two to three days, and a Retaliatory Attack authorised by the council. Repeated hits on the same city in one sitting land here, not on the first-time tier — and zeroing a member starts here.

2

Attacking a NAP member's RSS tile outside of All Out

Marches out gathering resources are protected. Tile hits on NAP members are only permitted during All Out — outside that window, hitting a gatherer is treated the same as hitting their city.

Resources returned to the victim, plus Demotion to R1 for one day.

Resources returned, Demotion to R1 for one day, plus an Arrogance Trial of one day.

Resources returned, Demotion to R1 for one day, plus an Indolence Trial of two days.

3

Attacking a non-NAP member's Town Centre more than twice

Players not covered by the pact may be hit, but no more than two times. Beyond that it becomes farming, and it is a violation — the limit exists so nobody gets driven off the kingdom.

Stop immediately, repay the player hit, and Demotion to R1 for one day. No further attacks on them.

Repayment, Demotion to R1 for one day, plus an Arrogance Trial of one day.

Repayment, Demotion to R1 for one day, plus an Avarice Trial of two days. Driving a non-NAP player off the kingdom is treated as malice.

4

Attacking NAP alliance structures, banners or buildings

Alliance banners, flags and built structures belonging to a NAP alliance are off limits. Do not attack them, and do not plant over them to force a fight.

Rebuild costs returned to the alliance, plus Demotion to R1 for one day.

Rebuild costs returned, Demotion to R1 for one day, plus an Arrogance Trial of one day.

Rebuild costs returned, Demotion to R1 for one day, an Avarice Trial of two days, and a Retaliatory Attack if it was aimed at one alliance.

5

Verbal, sexual or racial harassment of any player

In any channel — world, alliance or direct message — and against any player, including players from other kingdoms. There is no warning tier for this one.

Expulsion from the alliance and an open-ended NAP Blacklist. No first-time tier, no trial, no warning. Report it in game as well — it breaks the game's own rules.

6

Misuse of the Minister of Justice position

The position exists to serve the kingdom, not the holder's alliance or friends. Using it to shield rule-breakers, to punish rivals, or to hand out favours is a violation by the holder and by whoever appointed them to do it.

Removal from the post and a formal request to the King for a replacement, plus Demotion to R1 for one day. Where the position was used for personal gain, Expulsion as well. The kingdom is told what happened.

7

Stealing a Fort or Sanctuary from the assigned list

If an objective is assigned to an alliance on the assignments board, no other NAP alliance takes it. Claims go through the council before the rotation, not by rally.

The objective is handed back at the next window, uncontested.

Handed back, plus an Arrogance Trial of one day. The alliance loses its next assignment.

Handed back, an Avarice Trial of two days, and the alliance loses its next two assignments.

8

Poaching members of other alliances by direct message

Do not DM another alliance's players to recruit them. Players are free to move on their own, and open recruitment in world chat is fine — going into someone else's roster is not.

The recruiting R5 is warned publicly, and any player moved by poaching is returned.

Demotion to R1 for one day for whoever did the recruiting.

Expulsion from the alliance. Systematically stripping another roster is not a recruitment style.

9

Harboring a NAP blacklisted member

Anyone on the blacklist may not be given a home in a NAP alliance — main, farm or academy — while their listing is active. Accepting them makes the alliance answerable for them.

The alliance has 24 hours to remove the listed player.

The alliance is removed from the NAP directory. It is no longer a NAP member and is not covered by the pact. The kingdom is informed.

10

Breaking the KvK rules we outline

We negotiate a fresh agreement with each new server every KvK window, so the terms change every matchup — what was allowed last KvK may be a violation this one. Check the current KvK ruling before the window opens and again before you march. Council instructions on targets, timings, shield calls and rally coordination are binding for the duration of the event.

Removal from the KvK alliance for the rest of the event, and repayment where the kingdom lost something by it.

An Indolence Trial of two days, and no place in the KvK alliance for the next window.

An Avarice Trial of three days and Expulsion. Costing the kingdom a KvK on purpose is as serious as it gets short of the blacklist.

11

Abusing the system to get around the rules

Leaving your alliance to make a hit and rejoining afterwards, or using a farm account to do what your main cannot, is treated as the original violation plus a deliberate attempt to evade it. Both accounts are held responsible, and so is the R5 who allowed it.

Expulsion and an open-ended NAP Blacklist for both accounts, main and farm. The underlying violation is answered at double, and the R5 who allowed it answers to the council.

What each trial does

Arrogance Trial
−40% resource production

Your Town Centre buildings generate secured resources far more slowly. It has nothing to do with build times.

Indolence Trial
−25% training speed

Troops come out of the barracks slower, so rebuilding what you spent takes longer than it should.

Avarice Trial
−10% training, research and construction speed

Three penalties at once rather than one: troops, research and building all slow down together, which is why it is kept for malicious cases.

When in doubt, don't. If you cannot tell whether a hit is allowed, ask in kingdom chat before you march. Nobody has ever been punished for asking.

What is still allowed

The NAP is an internal truce, not a peace treaty with the world. All of the following are fair game:

Kingdom vs Kingdom

Players from other kingdoms — but only within the terms agreed for that matchup. See the current KvK ruling.

All Out

RSS tile hits on NAP members are permitted during All Out, and only then. Town Centres stay protected throughout. See the All Out page.

Non-NAP players

Up to two Town Centre attacks each. Their tiles and marches are open, but the TC limit is firm.

Bandits and beasts

All PvE targets on the map. Rally them freely — just don't cut across someone else's rally.

Arena

Hitting a K1886 player in Arena is never a violation. You are asked to use your refreshes to find targets from other kingdoms first — but once your refreshes are gone, hit a K1886 player rather than spend gems on more.

Event combat

Anything else the game itself puts kingdom members against each other in. Nothing that happens inside carries over onto the map.

Also expected of you

These are not on the violation list, but they are how the pact stays workable — ignore them often enough and you will end up committing a violation anyway.

  • Never retaliate before reporting and getting approval. If you are hit, screenshot it and send it up. Hitting back on your own is a violation of rule 1 in its own right — wait for the R5s to rule, and act only if they approve it.
  • Tiles are first come, first served. If a tile is already being gathered, take another one.
  • R5s answer for their alliance, including their farm and academy alliances.

Reporting a violation

  1. For an attack, share the battle report with your R4 or R5. It must show the attacker's name, their alliance tag and the timestamp.
  2. For chat or messages, screenshot the whole thing — the messages and the chat log around them, not one cropped line. Context is what decides these cases.
  3. For anything involving chat, include the account ID. Open the player's profile, screenshot it, and send the profile and account ID with the report. Names get changed the moment someone is reported; account IDs do not.
  4. Send it all to your own R4 or R5. Do not post it in world chat.
  5. Your R5 contacts the other alliance's R5. Most cases end here, with troops healed and resources returned.
  6. If unresolved, it goes to the council — the R5s of the NAP alliances, together with the Minister of Justice, who rule on it and set the punishment.

Report it while it is fresh. There is no deadline, but evidence goes stale fast — battle reports scroll away, chat gets buried, and people stop agreeing on what happened. A punishment handed down long after the event feels unfair to whoever receives it and does far less to stop the behaviour, so the sooner it is raised, the better it works for everyone.

Punishments

The council chooses from the fixed list below. Which punishment applies depends less on which rule was broken than on how it was broken — an honest mistake and a planned attack on the same player are not the same case.

How a case is weighed

  1. First violation, or an honest mistake An apology and putting it right is usually enough. Resources returned to the victim, troops healed, and that is the end of it. Nobody is trialled for a misread map. Attacks are the exception: any violation that involved attacking another player carries a one-day demotion to R1 as well, first time or not.
  2. Second violation The same thing twice is not a mistake. Repayment still applies, and a trial is added on top — short to begin with, longer if it happens again.
  3. Malicious intent, or several violations at once Planned, repeated, or aimed at one player to drive them out — and anything done over and over in one go. Five hits on the same Town Centre is one malicious case, not five first offences. Several different violations in one night are judged together, at the severity of the worst of them. This tier skips the ladder: the council starts well up the list whether or not there is anything on record.
  4. Severe violations have no ladder Harassment, spying for another kingdom, using a farm or an alliance swap to get around the rules, and zeroing a NAP member go straight to the top of the list. There is no first-time tier for these and no working up to it.

Repayment is not a punishment. Returning what you took puts the victim back where they were — it does not answer for the violation itself. Repaying quickly and without being chased is, however, the single thing most likely to keep a case at the bottom of this list.

What the blacklist means

Blacklisting means one thing: the player may not be accepted into any NAP alliance while the listing is active — not the main alliance, not a farm, not an academy. Taking one in is a violation by the alliance that does it (rule 9).

It changes nothing about how they may be attacked. A blacklisted player is treated as a non-NAP member and keeps the same protections as any other non-NAP member, including the limit of two Town Centre attacks in rule 3. Listings run for a set number of days, or open-ended until the council votes to lift them. Current listings are on the blacklist tracker.

Appeals

Any player may appeal through their R5 within 48 hours of a ruling. The council reviews the evidence again and votes; a simple majority of NAP R5s overturns or reduces the punishment. Rulings are not reopened after that.
